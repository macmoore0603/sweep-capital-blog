ES Futures Daily Recap: How to Read the Session Like a Pro

Sweep Capital Group • July 2026
Last updated: July 2026

Every trading day, the S&P 500 E-mini futures (ES) leaves a footprint. A story of where liquidity was taken, where institutions positioned, and where price was delivered.

Most traders see a daily candle and think "green day" or "red day." That's like looking at a book cover and saying "good" or "bad." You're missing the entire story inside.

This guide shows you how to read the ES session recap the way a professional does — what to look for, what to journal, and how today's session informs tomorrow's plan.

Why a Daily Recap Matters

The daily recap is not about yesterday. It's about tomorrow.

When you review the session — marking the sweep, the displacement, the distribution — you build a map. Tomorrow's session will interact with levels created today. If you don't know where today's liquidity was taken, you can't predict where tomorrow's will be.

Professional traders spend 30+ minutes after the close reviewing the session. Retail traders close their laptops and check Reddit.

The 5-Step ES Session Recap Framework

Step 1: Mark the Session Range

After the close, pull up your 5-minute ES chart. Mark:

  • Overnight high and low (the range formed during Asian + London sessions)
  • NY session high and low (the range after 9:30 AM ET)
  • Daily high and low (the full 24-hour range)

Draw horizontal lines at each. These are your reference levels.

Step 2: Identify Where Liquidity Was Taken

Look at each level and ask: did price sweep through it?

  • If the overnight high was exceeded at the NY open — buy stops were taken there
  • If the NY session low was swept late in the day — sell stops were taken there

Mark each sweep with a small arrow. This tells you which side of liquidity was manipulated.

Step 3: Identify the Displacement

After the sweep, did price reverse with a displacement candle? Or did it continue?

  • Reversal displacement = the sweep was a manipulation. The real move was in the opposite direction.
  • Continuation = the sweep was a real breakout. Price kept going.

Note the direction and magnitude of the displacement candle. How many points did it move? Was it larger than the average 5-minute candle?

Step 4: Map the Distribution

Where did price go after the displacement?

  • If the manipulation was to the upside (buy stops taken), distribution should be downward — toward the session low
  • If manipulation was to the downside (sell stops taken), distribution should be upward — toward the session high

Mark the target that was hit. Did price reach the opposite liquidity pool? Did it stop short?

Step 5: Journal the Setup

Write down:

  • Which liquidity level was swept
  • Time of sweep
  • Displacement direction and size
  • Distribution target
  • Whether the setup was tradable (did you get a clean entry signal?)
  • If you traded it: entry, stop, target, outcome
  • If you didn't trade it: why not?

This journal becomes your most valuable trading asset over time.

Example: ES Session Recap — July 24, 2026

Session data:
  • ES close: 7469.75
  • Session range: 65.0 points
  • Displacement: 19.25 points (0.26%)
Recap analysis:
  • Overnight high was 7480.25. At the NY open, price ran to 7482.50 — sweeping 2.25 points above the overnight high. Buy stops were taken.
  • A bearish displacement candle formed on the 5-minute chart, closing back below 7480. This confirmed the sweep was manipulation, not a breakout.
  • Price distributed downward toward the session low area at 7455.25. The move was 25 points from the sweep high to the session low — a clean Power of Three (AMD) session.
  • Entry signal: 5-15 gap retest at 7478. Stop at 7483 (above sweep). Target: 7460 (midpoint).
  • Result: +18 points from entry to target. $900 per contract.
Tomorrow's plan: The session low at 7455.25 is now a reference liquidity level. If price sweeps below it at the NY open, the same setup may print in reverse — a buy-side sweep followed by upward distribution toward 7480.

The Weekly Pattern

After journaling 5 sessions, patterns emerge:

  • Which side of liquidity gets swept most often at the NY open?
  • How far does price typically run after the sweep?
  • Which days of the week produce the cleanest setups?

Most traders never collect this data. They trade the same mistakes for years without realizing the answers are sitting in their journals.
