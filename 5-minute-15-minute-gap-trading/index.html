5-Minute 15-Minute Gap Trading: The ICT Entry Model

Sweep Capital Group • July 2026
Last updated: July 2026

If you've studied ICT concepts, you've heard of the 5-15 gap. It's one of the cleanest entry models in the Smart Money Concepts toolkit — a displacement gap on the 5-minute and 15-minute timeframes that marks where aggressive institutional order flow left a footprint.

The gap tells you exactly where institutions accumulated positions. The retest tells you where to enter.

This guide covers exactly how to identify the 5-15 gap, trade it with defined risk, and avoid the mistakes that cause most traders to misread it.

What the 5-15 Gap Is NOT

The 5-15 gap is not a "candle gap" like the ones you see on a daily chart. It's not a gap between any two random candles. And crucially, it's not a pattern you trade in isolation.

The 5-15 gap is specifically:

  • Found on the 5-minute and 15-minute timeframes
  • Created by aggressive displacement — candles that ignore price overlaps and close aggressively in one direction
  • Only valid when paired with a liquidity sweep or market structure shift context

If you're marking every gap between every 5-minute candle as a "5-15 setup," you're not trading ICT — you're gambling.

What the 5-15 Gap Actually Is

When a large institution or a pool of institutions enters the market aggressively, their orders consume liquidity across price levels so quickly that the 5-minute and 15-minute charts show a "gap" — candles that don't overlap with the previous candle, closing far away from where they opened.

After this displacement, the market often retests the gap area. This retest is the entry opportunity:

  • Displacement creates the gap
  • Price returns and retests the gap
  • With the right confirmation, this retest is where you enter

The gap acts as a support or resistance zone. It's not a random level — it's the footprint of where real money entered the market.

Step-by-Step: Trading the 5-15 Gap

Step 1: Identify Session Liquidity Levels

Before you look for the 5-15, you need the bigger picture. Mark the prior session high and low on the 5-minute or 15-minute chart. This is your liquidity context.

Step 2: Wait for a Sweep + Displacement

Watch for price to sweep one of your marked session levels. After the sweep, look for a displacement candle — a candle on the 5-minute chart that:

  • Closes beyond the session level
  • Leaves a gap between its close and the next candle's open (or close and the candle two bars back)
  • Is bigger than the average candle for that session

Step 3: Draw the Fair Value Gap (FVG)

The FVG is the gap between:

  • The close of the displacement candle, and
  • The open of the candle from the prior price range

Draw this zone. It represents the area where institutions filled their orders. Retails sellers were swept. The smart money bought/sold here.

Step 4: Wait for the Retest

Price should retrace toward the FVG zone. When it does:

  • Do NOT enter until price touches the zone
  • Do NOT enter if price reverses before testing the zone

Step 5: Entry

Enter when:

  • Price tests the FVG zone
  • You see a rejection candle (a small wick that can't get through the zone)
  • Or confirm with a secondary trigger, like a candlestick reversal on the 1-minute chart

Step 6: Risk and Target

  • Stop loss: On the other side of the displacement candle (above for shorts, below for longs). Tight stops are tested quickly — use at least 4–6 ticks on ES.
  • Target: The next session's liquidity level (the high or low that was not swept yet)

Common Failure Mode: Entering Every Gap

Not every gap on the 5-minute chart is a 5-15 entry. Gaps form constantly. The ones you trade must occur:

  • Around a session level lodging liquidity
  • After a sweep
  • With a pictureable rejection or confirmation

Trading every gap you see is noise trading — you will lose money on random entries that have no larger context. Quality matters more than frequency.

A Recent 5-15 Gap Trade

On ES futures in mid-July:

  • London session high was 7445. NY session
  • Price swept London high at the open (toward 7450), closed outside it, and left a 5-point gap on the 15-minute chart.
  • The FVG was drawn from 7445 to 7449.
  • Price retraced back 20 minutes later and retested the 7445–7449 zone
  • Entry at 7447 with a stop at 7452 (+5 ticks risk)
  • Target: the NY session low area
  • Risk: $250. Reward: $650 before the session low hit.

This was one complete 5-15 trade — sweep, displacement, gap, retest, entry, target.

Risk Rules

  • Only trade 5-15 gaps that form after a sweep of session liquidity.
  • Wait for the retest — entering on the displacement candle itself produces bad entries.
  • Never risk more than 1–2% of the account per trade.
  • If the gap is wider than 10–12 ticks on ES, it's too wide for a reliable retest — skip it.
  • One 5-15 gap per session maximum. If the first one fails, no second attempts.
