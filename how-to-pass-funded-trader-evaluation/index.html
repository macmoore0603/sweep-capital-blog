What Is a Funded Trader Evaluation?

A funded trader evaluation (also called a "combine," "challenge," or "evaluation phase") is a risk-controlled test that proprietary trading firms use to screen traders before granting access to firm capital.

The typical structure:

  • Account size: $25K, $50K, $100K, or $200K
  • Profit target: Usually 8-10% of the account size
  • Max drawdown: 5-10% (daily or total)
  • Time limit: 30 days, 60 days, or unlimited
  • Minimum trading days: Usually 3-5 days
  • Cost: $25-$500 depending on account size

If you hit the profit target without hitting the drawdown limit, you get a funded account. If you hit the drawdown limit at any point, you fail and lose your evaluation fee.

Why Most Traders Fail the Evaluation

The #1 reason traders fail evaluations is not lack of skill. It's lack of risk discipline.

Here's what happens: A trader who normally risks 1-2% per trade on their own account gets a $100K evaluation account and suddenly starts risking 3-5% per trade. The size of the number on the screen changes their behavior.

Top 5 Failure Modes

  • Overleveraging early. You lose 2 trades in a row at 3% each, and suddenly you're at -6%. Half your drawdown limit gone in an hour.
  • Revenge trading. You take a loss, immediately re-enter in the same direction "to get it back," and now you're at -8% with a single bad sequence.
  • Trading too many instruments. You open positions on ES, NQ, CL, GC, and EUR/USD simultaneously. When the market rotates, everything correlates and you blow through your daily loss limit.
  • Ignoring the time limit. You're conservative for 25 days, sitting at +3%, and then panic-trade the last 5 days to chase the 8% target.
  • No defined entry model. You're trading "setups" that are really just vibes — no invalidation level, no defined risk, no specific trigger. When the evaluation pressure is on, undefined entries become gambling.

The Sweep Capital Framework for Passing Evaluations

Not all prop firms are equal. Here's what to look for:

Pay attention to the consistency rule — some firms require that no single day contributes more than 30-40% of your total profit. This prevents lottery-ticket trading but also limits your ability to capitalize on high-conviction setups.

Step 2: Risk 0.5-1% Per Trade. Not More.

The math here is simple. If your evaluation has a 5% max drawdown and you risk 1% per trade:

  • You can lose 5 trades in a row before hitting the drawdown limit
  • With a 50% win rate, the probability of 5 consecutive losses is 3.1%
  • With a 40% win rate, it's 7.8%

If you risk 2% per trade:

  • You can only lose 2.5 trades before failing
  • With a 50% win rate, 2 consecutive losses happen 25% of the time
  • With a 40% win rate, 2 consecutive losses happen 36% of the time
Risk less, survive longer, pass more often.

Step 3: Trade One Instrument

Pick one instrument for the entire evaluation. We recommend ES (E-mini S&P 500 futures) or NQ (E-mini Nasdaq futures) because:

  • Instagram: the most liquidity during US session hours
  • Clear market structure on 5/15-minute timeframes
  • Predictable session behavior (London sweep, NY open, NY close)
  • Less gap risk than individual stocks

When you trade one instrument, you learn its personality — how it reacts to news, how it behaves at session opens, where liquidity pools sit. This instrument-specific knowledge is what gets you through the evaluation.

Step 4: Use a Defined Entry Model — Every Single Trade

This is where we see most evaluations fail. Traders enter the market without a specific reason, then hold and hope.

At Sweep Capital, we teach the Session Sweep model:

  • Identify prior session high and low (liquidity levels)
  • Wait for price to sweep one of these levels (take the liquidity)
  • Wait for a market structure shift (confirmation that the sweep is complete)
  • Enter on the retest with defined risk below the sweep candle
  • Target the opposite session's liquidity

This model works for evaluations because every trade has:

  • A defined entry trigger (sweep + structure shift + retest)
  • A defined invalidation (below the sweep candle)
  • A defined target (opposite liquidity)
  • A risk-to-reward ratio of at least 1:2

Step 5: Maximum 2 Trades Per Day

The evaluation is a marathon, not a sprint. Limiting yourself to 2 trades per day:

  • Prevents overtrading and revenge trading
  • Forces you to wait for high-quality setups
  • Keeps your emotions stable across the evaluation period
  • Reduces commission costs

If both trades stop out, you're done for the day. No exceptions.

Step 6: Journal Every Trade

Log every trade with:

  • Entry screenshot at time of execution
  • Exit screenshot at time of close
  • Why you entered (which model, which level, which trigger)
  • What you did wrong (always something)
  • Risk amount and R:R
  • Emotional state (calm, anxious, revenge, confident)

This journal is your proof to yourself that you traded your plan. If you fail the evaluation, the journal tells you exactly why — and what to fix before the next attempt.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

"I'll trade more aggressively to finish faster"

No. The evaluation doesn't reward speed. It rewards consistency. We've seen traders pass in 4 days and we've seen them pass in 28 days. The ones who take 4 days traded the exact same risk model as the ones who took 28 — the market just gave them more setups.

"I'll skip the evaluation and go straight to live trading"

If you can't pass a risk-controlled evaluation, you won't survive live trading. The evaluation is easier than live trading because the evaluation has guardrails. Live trading doesn't.

"I need a bigger account to make real money"

A $50K funded account paying 80-90% profit split generates $4,000-$4,500 per 10% gain. If you can consistently make 5-10% per month (which is what evaluations test for), you're making $2,000-$4,500/month on a $50K account. That's real money.

If You Keep Failing Evaluations

If you've failed 2+ evaluations, the problem isn't the evaluation. The problem is one of:

  • No defined entry model — you're trading random setups
  • No risk discipline — you're overleveraging under pressure
  • No instrument specialization — you're jumping between markets
  • Emotional trading — you're revenge trading after losses

Any of these is fixable with structured mentorship.
