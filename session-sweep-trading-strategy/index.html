Session Sweep Trading Strategy: The Complete Guide

Sweep Capital Group • July 2026
Last updated: July 2026

You've watched the YouTube videos. You know the pattern: price pushes into a prior session's high or low, sweeps the liquidity, then reverses. You see it on charts after the fact and think "I should have taken that trade."

But in real time, when the sweep hits and price moves fast, you freeze. You don't know whether the sweep is complete or whether it's going to keep running. You enter late, your stop gets hit, and you watch the very move you predicted play out without you.

This guide breaks down the Session Sweep trading strategy exactly — what it is, why it works, and the step-by-step process to trade it with defined risk.

What the Session Sweep Is NOT

The Session Sweep is not a signal. It's not "see a wick above the high and short it." That's how retail traders lose money — they enter on the sweep without waiting for the reversal signal.

It's also not a "always works" pattern. Some sweeps are real breakouts disguised as sweeps. Differentiating between a genuine sweep (that reverses) and a continuation breakout (that keeps running) is the core skill — and what mentorship teaches you.

The Session Sweep is not exclusive to ICT. ICT named it and popularized it, but the underlying concept — that institutions need to fill orders at key levels before they can move price in the opposite direction — is how markets have always worked. Smart money concepts describe behavior that's been visible on charts for decades.

What the Session Sweep Actually Is

The Session Sweep is a three-step process:

  • Map liquidity. Draw the prior session's high and low. Above the high are buy stop orders (retail traders betting on a breakout). Below the low are sell stop orders (traders expecting a breakdown).
  • Wait for the sweep. Price moves into these levels and triggers the stop orders. This is the "liquidity grab" — institutions need these orders filled to execute their real positions.
  • Identify the structure shift. After the sweep, price should show a clear rejection — a sharp reversal candle, a structure break on a lower timeframe, or a displacement candle that closes back within the prior session's range. This confirms the sweep, not a breakout.

Step-by-Step: How to Trade the Session Sweep

Step 1: Define the Session (Pre-Market)

Before the market opens, mark these levels on your chart:

  • Asian session high and low (for pre-London sessions)
  • London session high and low (for the NY open)
  • Prior NY session high and low (for the afternoon session)

Label them with horizontal lines. These are your liquidity pools.

Step 2: Wait for Price to Enter the Zone

This is the hardest part. You wait.

Price will approach one of these levels. You do not act yet. Watch for price to push through the level (sweep the liquidity) — the wick should extend beyond your line by at least a few ticks.

Step 3: Wait for Confirmation (NOT Entry)

The sweep is not the entry. The entry comes from confirmation:

Confirmation signals (on 1-3 minute timeframe):
  • A displacement candle closing back on the inside of the range
  • A market structure shift (MSS) — the first candle's low being taken after the sweep
  • A fair value gap (FVG) that price retests after the sweep

If the sweep runs 5+ candles past the level with no reversal, it's not a sweep — it's a breakout. Do not enter.

Step 4: Entry

Entry:

  • On the retest of the displacement candle or the FVG
  • In the direction toward the opposite session's liquidity

Step 5: Risk and Targets

  • Stop loss: below/above the sweep candle's wick (the candle that took the liquidity)
  • Target 1: The midpoint of the session range
  • Target 2: The opposite session's high/low area

A good Session Sweep setup should have at least a 1:2 risk-to-reward ratio.

Common Failure Mode: Entering Too Early

The #1 mistake traders make with the Session Sweep is entering on the sweep candle itself — before confirmation.

When you enter on the sweep:

  • You don't know if this is a genuine sweep or a breakout
  • Your stop is immediately tested because the candle is still moving
  • You're trading hope, not process

Wait for the confirmation candle. If you don't get one, the trade didn't set up. Move on.

Risk Rules

  • Maximum risk per trade: 1-2% of your account. If you're trading a $10,000 account, that's exactly one E-mini contract with a 6-point stop ($300).
  • Daily loss limit: 3% of your account. If you hit this, you're done for the day.
  • Maximum 2 Session Sweep entries per day. Wait for only the highest-quality setups.

If you follow these rules and the trade doesn't work, you live to trade the next session. If you break the rules, one bad session can end your account.
